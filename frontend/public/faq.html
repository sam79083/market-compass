Frequently asked

Questions about the dip check.

Short answers to the things readers ask most often. The thresholds are on the methodology page.

What is Market Compass?

A free, static page that shows four public readings of market stress for the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ: panic level (VIX or VXN), an oversold check (RSI), the 200-day trend, and the CNN Fear & Greed index, each described in plain words.

Is this investment advice?

No. It is an informational tool. It does not know your goals, time horizon, taxes, or risk tolerance, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. See the terms and disclaimer.

Does it tell me when to buy?

No. It describes how much current conditions look like a dip. Stress readings can stay high, or get worse, after a first drop, and none of these measures can find the bottom. Many investors buy in stages instead of at a single point.

What do the dots mean?

One dot means a mild reading, two a clear one, and three an extreme one. No dots means the reading does not look like a dip. The dots describe the reading, not a prediction.

Why do all four often say the same thing?

Fear, oversold prices, and a falling index tend to happen together, and the Fear & Greed index even includes volatility and momentum among its inputs. When all four agree, treat it as one strong message rather than four independent confirmations.

Why is the 200-day line included?

It helps separate a normal pullback inside an uptrend from a deeper decline. Measured on daily closes since 2006, both indexes have been below their 200-day average about 1 day in 5. A pullback of 2% to 10% below it is common; a drop of more than 10% below it is the kind of decline seen in bear markets.

How often is the data updated?

A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow runs on weekday evenings (U.S. market days) and publishes new static JSON snapshots. Each card shows the date of its latest reading.

Where does the data come from?

Cboe (VIX), Cboe via Yahoo Finance (VXN), Yahoo Finance’s public chart interface for index closes used in RSI and the 200-day trend, and CNN for Fear & Greed. Some feeds are unofficial and can be delayed.

Why is a reading sometimes missing?

If a feed fails, the page shows “No data” for that card. It does not substitute a different metric.
