Frequently asked

Questions about the compass.

Short answers to the things readers ask most often. The full rules are on the methodology page.

What is Market Compass?

A free, static dashboard that combines public U.S. equity valuation, sentiment, trend, and breadth indicators into a 0–100 relative-attractiveness score. It also publishes the underlying history, weights, data-quality flags, and validation output.

Is this investment advice?

No. It is an informational research tool. It does not know your goals, time horizon, taxes, or risk tolerance, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. See the terms and disclaimer.

What does the 0–100 score mean?

Higher means conditions have historically been more favorable for long-term buyers; lower means more expensive or more stretched. The score is a percentile-like summary, not a probability and not a price target. Read the bands and weights.

Why does the market often look “Very Overvalued”?

Valuation measures such as CAPE are slow-moving and have spent long stretches above their long-run averages. A low score can persist for years. The score describes the current reading relative to history; it does not claim the market must fall next month.

How often is the data updated?

A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow runs on weekday evenings (U.S. market days) and publishes new static JSON snapshots. Some inputs are monthly or annual and carry publication lags, so not every indicator changes every day. The dashboard shows the as-of date and freshness for each indicator.

Where does the data come from?

Shiller CAPE and earnings (shillerdata.com), Cboe VIX, U.S. Treasury yields, World Bank market-cap/GDP, Yahoo Finance’s public chart interface for S&P 500 and ETF prices, and a public S&P 500 constituent dataset. The methodology lists the lags and limitations.

Why are Forward P/E and CNN Fear & Greed sometimes unavailable?

Both require licensed or authorized feeds that are not bundled with the project. The dashboard does not substitute a different metric and relabel it. It shows the indicator as unavailable, lowers coverage, and may lower confidence. A separate VIX/RSI sentiment proxy is labeled explicitly.

What does “confidence” mean?

Confidence reflects coverage and data quality, not the direction of the score. High confidence requires at least 90% coverage and no quality flags; medium requires at least 70%. A high-confidence score can still be wrong about the future.

Does the history use hindsight?

No. Each observation has an effective date that includes a conservative publication lag. A score for a given date uses only observations that would have been available by then. The newest preliminary CAPE month is excluded until its lag has elapsed.

Why does breadth have survivorship bias?

The breadth series uses the current S&P 500 constituent list for the entire history, because a free point-in-time membership history is not bundled. That makes the historical breadth reading somewhat flattering. The dashboard flags this explicitly.

Why are there ads?

Hosting and domain costs are real. Ads, when enabled, help cover them. They are separated from the analysis and do not influence the methodology, rankings, or data. There are no sponsored scores or paid placements. See the privacy policy for advertising and consent details.

Can I reuse the code or data?

The source repository is public: github.com/sam79083/market-compass. Review the licenses and terms of the underlying data providers before redistributing their data. The project’s own code is intended for inspection and learning.
