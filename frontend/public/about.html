About the project

A transparent valuation compass for the U.S. stock market.

Market Compass turns ten public market signals into one 0–100 relative-attractiveness score, then shows its work: the raw data, the percentile windows, the weights, the missing inputs, and the historical record.

Point-in-time by design

Each score uses only information that would have been available on that date, including publication lags. The history is not rebuilt with hindsight.

Missing data stays missing

Unavailable indicators are disclosed and excluded, never silently replaced with a different metric. Coverage and confidence are shown next to the score.

Open and reproducible

The scoring code, weights, and validation output are public. A weights hash lets you confirm which configuration produced a snapshot.

What the dashboard shows

The composite combines four categories: fundamental valuation, relative attractiveness versus Treasuries, sentiment and momentum, and market breadth. Each underlying indicator is converted to a 0–100 score against its own rolling history. Higher scores mean conditions have historically been more favorable; lower scores mean more expensive or more stretched conditions.

The score is a description of the present, not a forecast. A market can stay expensive for years, and a cheap market can get cheaper. The validation page shows what forward returns have looked like after similar readings, including how often the signal was early or wrong.

Why it exists

Most valuation dashboards hide their assumptions behind a single number. Market Compass was built to make the assumptions inspectable: which data is stale, which indicator is a proxy, which history has survivorship bias, and how much of the composite is actually covered.

It is also a deliberately small project. There is no account, no portfolio tracking, no trading integration, and no paywall. The goal is a useful public reference, not a signal service.

Data and refresh schedule

The site is a static snapshot. A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow refreshes the data on weekday evenings (U.S. market days), recomputes the scores, and publishes new JSON files. The page you are reading is served from the same static build.

  • Shiller CAPE and earnings: Robert Shiller / shillerdata.com.
  • VIX: Cboe Global Markets.
  • 10-year Treasury yield: U.S. Department of the Treasury.
  • Market capitalization / GDP: World Bank.
  • S&P 500 prices and ETFs: Yahoo Finance’s public chart interface.
  • S&P 500 constituents: public constituent dataset on GitHub.

Some feeds are unofficial or have publication lags. Forward P/E and CNN Fear & Greed require licensed or authorized feeds and stay marked unavailable until configured. Read the methodology and the data notes on the dashboard before using a score.

Who it is for

Individual investors, students, journalists, and researchers who want a transparent starting point for thinking about long-term market conditions. It is not a substitute for a financial adviser, a licensed data terminal, or your own risk tolerance.

It is not intended for high-frequency trading, professional investment advice, or decisions that require real-time or licensed data. See the terms and disclaimer.

Independence and funding

Market Compass is independently run. There are no sponsored rankings, paid placements, or affiliate recommendations. The site may display advertising to cover domain and hosting costs; ads are separated from the analysis and do not influence the methodology.
