Methodology

How the readings are labeled.

Every label on the page comes from a fixed rule listed here. There is no composite score and no weighting between readings.

These readings describe current market conditions. They are not a forecast, and they do not tell you when to buy or sell or account for your time horizon, taxes, or risk tolerance.

1. The four readings

Reading What it is Source
Panic level VIX for the S&P 500, VXN for the NASDAQ-100: the market’s expected 30-day volatility. Cboe
Oversold check RSI, 14 days, Wilder’s smoothing, on the index’s daily closes. Calculated from Yahoo Finance closes
200-day trend How far the latest close is above or below its 200-trading-day simple moving average, in percent. Calculated from Yahoo Finance closes
Fear & Greed CNN’s 0 to 100 index of seven market measures, 0 being extreme fear. CNN Business

2. Labels and dots

Each reading falls into a labeled range. The dots show how much that range looks like a dip: no dots for none, one for mild, two for clear, three for extreme.

Reading Ranges and labels
VIX / VXN Below 20 Calm (0) · 20 to 30 Elevated (1) · 30 to 40 High fear (2) · 40 and above Extreme panic (3)
RSI (14) 70 and above Overbought (0) · 60 to 70 Strong (0) · 40 to 60 Neutral (0) · 30 to 40 Getting oversold (1) · 20 to 30 Oversold (2) · below 20 Deeply oversold (3)
200-day trend More than 2% above Uptrend (0) · within 2% Testing the line (1) · 2% to 10% below Pullback (2) · more than 10% below Bear territory (3)
Fear & Greed 76 to 100 Extreme greed (0) · 56 to 75 Greed (0) · 45 to 55 Neutral (0) · 25 to 44 Fear (1) · 0 to 24 Extreme fear (3)

These cutoffs are common round numbers, chosen for readability. They are not statistical guarantees. The “below this line about 1 day in 5” line on the 200-day card is a measured figure from daily closes since 2006 (about 22% of days for both the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ Composite); the “higher than X% of days” line on the VIX card is the current reading’s percentile within its own 20-year history.

3. Data freshness

A scheduled GitHub Actions workflow refreshes the data on weekday evenings (U.S. market days) and publishes static JSON files. Each card shows the date of its latest reading. If a feed fails, the card shows “No data”; another metric is never substituted.

4. Ways the result can be wrong

  • The readings overlap. They usually rise and fall together, so agreement is weaker evidence than it looks.
  • A high-stress reading can persist or worsen. None of these measures can identify a market bottom.
  • Some feeds are unofficial and can be delayed, revised, or briefly unavailable.
  • The 200-day line separates a normal pullback from a deep decline only in hindsight.
